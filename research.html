Research

From a clinical need to a working robot

After a stroke, many people lose control of the ankle and toes, which makes walking and standing harder. I design portable robots that train both, and evaluate them with the clinicians who will use them.

ATOBOT: an underactuated ankle–toe rehabilitation robot

PhD research · 2022 – present · Funded by Portescap India CSR

2-DOFunderactuated, cable-driven
24-bitload-cell ADC (ADS1261)
2400encoder counts per revolution
N = 40clinicians in the evaluation

ATOBOT is a portable robot for post-stroke foot function training, designed to work with patients lying, sitting and standing.

  • Sensing and control. Teensy firmware, an ADS1261 load-cell ADC for force sensing, optical encoder feedback, and PID control with minimum-jerk trajectories.
  • Modelling. Gravity compensation fitted from footplate sweep experiments in Python, with residual analysis to separate friction effects.
  • Clinical evaluation. A study with 40 rehabilitation professionals, analysed with Mann–Whitney U tests and thematic analysis, which identified five priorities for the next design: standing training, toe training, control strategy, patient-specific adaptation and game design.
  • Next steps. A usability study based on the System Usability Scale (IRB submission in preparation), and a PROSPERO systematic review of robot-assisted ankle rehabilitation after stroke.

N400 ERP-based assessment of motor learning

Master’s thesis · Jadavpur University · 2020 – 2022

I proposed using the N400 event-related potential, recorded with EEG, as a marker of how well a person learns new motor tasks, and built a TSK Type-2 fuzzy classifier that groups subjects into high, medium and low learning adaptability.

Bad data detection and correction in smart grids

Undergraduate project · B.Tech in Electrical Engineering · 2016 – 2020

  • Developed a computational intelligence–based approach to detect and correct corrupted data injected into smart grid networks.
  • Implemented the approach using MATLAB and C.
