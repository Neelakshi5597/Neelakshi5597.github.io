Case study: I have measured how the medium changes the message

I showed my robot to 40 rehabilitation professionals. Twenty handled it in person; twenty watched a video demonstration online. Then I asked them to rate six statements about it.

65% → 94.7%

Agreement with the same control idea, in-person versus video. It was the only item where the two groups differed after a strict statistical correction.

An honest caveat, the kind I would also give in an edit review: the video group also had more clinical experience, so the format is a likely driver, not a proven one. The lesson I took is a content one. How people meet an idea shapes what they feel about it, so plan the medium on purpose and test it.

The second half of the study was listening. I coded every open comment into five themes, from standing training to game design. That is audience research: hearing what people want in their own words, then building the next version around it.

The eye: off the lab bench

Ankles are my thing. I build a robot that gets them moving again after a stroke, and I test my own on the dance floor.

I train in Bengali classical dance through the Bangiya Sangeet Parishad examinations: Rabindra Nritya (fifth year passed with First Division, sixth year in progress) and a Nazrul Nritya Junior Diploma.

Dance taught me what a camera needs: rhythm, framing of hands and feet, how a still pose and a moving one tell different stories, and how adornment catches light only when the body moves. That is a lot of what makes jewellery content work.

It also taught me to be on the other side of the lens, which helps when directing talent.
