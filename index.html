About

My PhD work, ATOBOT, is a portable ankle–toe robot for people recovering from stroke, now protected by a granted Indian patent.

I am an electrical and biomedical engineer in the joint PhD programme of IIT Madras, Christian Medical College Vellore and SCTIMST. My work spans sensing and control electronics, embedded firmware, data analysis and clinical evaluation. Before the PhD, I spent two years at Cognizant as an SAP consultant in client-facing delivery.

Research

2022 – present

ATOBOT: an ankle–toe rehabilitation robot for post-stroke foot function training

PhD research · IIT Madras / CMC Vellore / SCTIMST · Funded by Portescap India CSR
2-DOFunderactuated, cable-driven
24-bitload-cell ADC (ADS1261)
2400encoder counts per revolution
N = 40clinicians in the evaluation
  • Took ATOBOT from a clinical need through concept and early prototype to a working system.
  • Built the sensing and control platform: Teensy firmware, ADS1261 load-cell ADC, optical encoder feedback, and PID control with minimum-jerk trajectories.
  • Modelled gravity compensation from footplate sweep experiments in Python, using SciPy curve fitting and residual analysis.
  • Ran a clinician-centred evaluation with 40 rehabilitation professionals: protocol design, Mann–Whitney U tests with Bonferroni correction, and thematic analysis that identified five priority areas for future development.
  • Preparing an IRB submission for the next-stage usability study, and a PROSPERO systematic review on robot-assisted ankle neurorehabilitation in stroke.
2020 – 2022

N400 ERP-based assessment of motor learning

Postgraduate thesis · Jadavpur University
  • Proposed using N400 event-related potentials as a neurophysiological marker of motor-learning ability.
  • Developed a TSK Type-2 fuzzy classifier that groups subjects into high, medium and low learning adaptability.
